🎮 Game / 游戏

Game

Video games are, to me, one of the most remarkable artistic mediums of our time. They bring together system design, visual art, music, narrative, and player interaction into a single experience. A great game is not only something to be watched or consumed, but something to be explored, understood, and mastered. That is why I happily regard games as the Ninth Art.

对我而言，电子游戏是当代最独特也最迷人的艺术形式之一。它把系统设计、视觉艺术、音乐、叙事与玩家互动融合进同一个体验之中。一款伟大的游戏不仅仅是被观看或被消费的作品，而是一段需要被探索、被理解、并最终被掌握的旅程。也正因为如此，我始终乐于把游戏视为一种艺术——第九艺术。

Here, when I talk about games, I am referring specifically to non-PvP games. I do not particularly enjoy games where the primary source of utility comes from competing with other players in terms of technical skill. While such games certainly contain elements of strategic enjoyment, the fact that only one side can ultimately win makes the experience largely zero-sum in nature. Moreover, the amount of time required to continuously refine one’s technical skills often feels disproportionate to the enjoyment gained. More importantly, these kinds of games rarely have a true stopping point. PvE games usually provide a clear sense of completion: defeating the final boss, earning a platinum trophy, or fully exploring the entire world. PvP games, on the other hand, seldom offer such an endpoint; their structure often becomes an endless repetition of one match after another. For example, PvP games I have played include Red Alert 2, StarCraft II, and Honor of Kings. In the long run, these games tend to devolve into the simple act of “playing another round,” which happens to be the kind of gaming state I dislike the most. It feels less like a meaningful experience and more like a way of passing time—almost a form of disrespect toward one’s own time. In contrast, PvE games feel more like a one-on-one dialogue between the player and the game’s creator. The player moves through a world designed by the author—exploring it, understanding it, and eventually completing it—much like finishing a film, reading a novel, or listening through an entire music album. It becomes a journey that can be remembered, retold, and even taken pride in. For this reason, I fully recognize that such video games can be regarded as an artistic medium—the Ninth Art.

这里的游戏指的是 non-PvP 游戏。我并不喜欢那种把游玩乐趣（utility）建立在与其他玩家技术比拼之上的游戏。虽然其中确实存在博弈的乐趣，但由于只能有一方获胜，本质上仍然是一种接近零和的体验。而为了在这种竞争中不断精进技术所投入的时间成本，与最终获得的乐趣相比，在我看来并不成比例。更重要的是，这类游戏往往没有一个真正的停止节点。PVE 游戏通常有明确的完成状态，比如打败最终 Boss、拿到白金奖杯、或者完整探索整个世界。而 PvP 游戏却很少有这样的终点，它们的结构往往只是不断重复的一局又一局。比如我曾经玩过的 PvP 游戏：《红色警戒 2》、《StarCraft II》、《王者荣耀》。这些游戏到最后往往都会变成一种纯粹“再开一局”的状态，而这种状态恰恰是我最不喜欢的游戏状态，因为它更像是一种时间消磨，是一种对自己时间的不尊重。相比之下，PVE 游戏更像是玩家与游戏作者之间的一场一对一对话。玩家沿着作者设计的世界前行、探索、理解并最终完成它，就像看完一部电影、读完一本小说、听完整张音乐专辑一样。这是一段可以回忆、可以讲述、甚至可以为之感到自豪的旅程。也正因为如此，我完全认可这类电子游戏可以被视为一种艺术形式——第九艺术。

In terms of game aesthetics, the part I enjoy the most and value the most is what I call “gameplay.” By gameplay, I mean a work first showing you its underlying logic, then allowing you to move from initial contact and experimentation to learning, familiarity, and mastery, and finally placing you on a stage carefully constructed by the creator where you can fully apply everything you have learned. Plants vs. Zombies is a very typical and elegant example. At the beginning, you only know the most basic rule: if zombies reach the final row, they eat your brain and the game ends. Then you learn to defend yourself by planting Peashooters, but soon realize that planting requires sunlight. Later you discover that Sunflowers increase sunlight production, and tactical awareness naturally emerges: in the early stage, you should maximize your economy as much as possible and establish the most stable and efficient defense line as early as you can. As the characteristics of each plant gradually unfold, the game constantly pushes you toward situations that are both more complex and more exciting. It is not merely increasing numerical pressure; it is expanding your understanding of the system of rules itself. By the time you reach those carefully designed later stages, the pleasure you experience is not simply the pleasure of “winning,” but the satisfaction of finally being able to apply a complete set of rules that you fully understand—smoothly and beautifully. Great gameplay requires that this entire process feel seamless: the rules must be introduced naturally, the learning curve must be clear, the growth in complexity must be well balanced, and the final stage must be sufficiently rewarding. Most importantly, every stage of the process must be enjoyable in itself. From first contact, to gradual mastery, to full expression, the experience should continuously engage and stimulate the player, rather than forcing them through tedious tutorials or repetitive actions that are not genuinely fun. Red Dead Redemption 2, in contrast, serves as a fitting counterexample. It possesses extraordinarily beautiful visuals, deeply immersive storytelling, and incredibly moving character writing. Even today, Arthur’s ending can still bring me to tears; and killing Micah in the snowy mountains remains the most satisfying moment of emotional release I have ever experienced in gaming. Yet if we focus solely on gameplay, I still find it relatively weak. Your core interactions with the world are actually quite limited: greeting people, activating Dead Eye to shoot people, or shooting animals. Its achievements in narrative, atmosphere, and immersion far exceed its achievements in systemic design and interactive depth. For that reason, although I love the game and fully acknowledge it as a great work, I have never personally labeled it a “must-play masterpiece.” Because for me, a truly great game must ultimately stand on the strength of playing itself.

而在游戏的审美上，我最享受、也最看重的，是我称作 “游戏性” 的东西。所谓“游戏性”，就是一个作品先向你展示它的运行逻辑，再让你从最初的接触与试探，逐渐走向学习、熟悉、精通，最后再把你放进一个作者精心搭建的舞台里，让你将已经掌握的一切完整地施展出来。拿《植物大战僵尸》来说，这就是一个非常典型、也非常优雅的例子。一开始，你只知道最基本的规则：僵尸一旦走到最后一排，就会吃掉你的脑子，游戏结束。接着，你学会通过种植豌豆射手来抵御僵尸，但很快又会发现，种植物需要阳光；再往后，你明白向日葵可以提高阳光产能，于是战术意识便自然形成：前期要尽可能把经济拉高，尽早建立起最稳定、最高效的防线。随着每一种植物的特性逐渐展开，游戏不断把你推向更复杂、也更精彩的局面。它不是单纯地增加数值压力，而是在不断扩展你对规则体系的理解。最后，当你来到那些被精心设计的关卡时，你获得的快感，并不只是“赢了”，而是你终于能够把自己已经完全理解的一整套规则，流畅而漂亮地运用出来。优秀的游戏性，就是这整个过程必须足够丝滑：它的规则展示要自然，学习曲线要清晰，复杂度的提升要合理，最后的舞台也要足够精彩。而更重要的是，每一个环节本身都必须足够有趣。从第一次接触，到逐步掌握，再到真正施展，整个过程都应该让玩家持续感到被吸引、被激发，而不是被迫忍受一段无聊的教学，或者机械地重复一些并不真正好玩的操作。反过来说，《荒野大镖客 2》是很恰当的反例。它拥有极其美丽的画面、无比沉浸的叙事，以及极其动人的角色塑造。直到今天，我依然会为亚瑟的结局潸然泪下；而在雪山上杀迈狗，那种情绪宣泄与复仇完成的瞬间，也是我整个游玩史中最痛快的一次。但如果只谈“游戏性”，我始终觉得它是相对薄弱的。你和这个世界的核心交互，其实相当有限：与人打招呼、开启死神之眼开枪打人、开枪打动物。它在叙事、氛围和沉浸感上的成就，远远高于它在规则设计和互动深度上的成就。也正因如此，虽然我非常喜欢它、也承认它是一部伟大的作品，但我始终没有把它贴上 “must-play masterpiece” 的标签。因为在我这里，一部真正的游戏杰作，必须在 “玩” 这件事本身上足够成立。
